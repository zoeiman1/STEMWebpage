Conferences, Professional & Community Activities

Conferences & Poster Presentations

  • • 2026 ERN Conference in STEM (Atlanta, GA): Four scholars attended and presented research results; Torrean Mozley won 2nd place in CS & Info Management[cite: 1, 3, 4, 5, 7, 8].
  • • 2026 Tuskegee JARS: Four scholars presented their undergraduate research projects at the Joint Annual Research Symposium[cite: 1, 5, 7, 8].
  • • 2025 AAAS S-STEM Meeting (San Diego, CA): Five scholars attended and presented a poster on freshman introductory coding seminars[cite: 1, 3, 5, 7].
  • • 2025 NSBP / NSHP Joint Annual (San Jose, CA): Two scholars attended; Kaiya Jones earned special cyber presentation recognition[cite: 1, 3, 4, 5, 7, 8].
  • • 2025 AAAI Fall Symposium Series: One scholar attended with the support of a competitive travel award[cite: 1, 5].
  • • 2025 ERN Conference (Atlanta, GA): Four scholars presented research projects; Kaiya Jones won 2nd place poster honors[cite: 3, 4, 5, 7, 8].

Career & Leadership Development

  • • Internship Experience Sharing: Upperclassmen scholars lead peer panels sharing industry internship takeaways, technical interview strategies, and workplace readiness[cite: 1, 5].
  • • Scholar-Led Coding Seminars: S-STEM scholars organize and teach introductory programming workshops for incoming Computer Science freshmen[cite: 3, 7].
  • • National Hackathons & Competitions: Scholars represent Tuskegee in national computing competitions, including 1st place finishes at the 2026 National HBCU Battle of the Brains and UIUC Precision Digital Ag Hackathon[cite: 1, 5].

Community Building & Milestones

  • • Conference Experience Sharing: Post-conference cohort debrief sessions allowing presenters to share reviewer insights and networking lessons with fellow scholars[cite: 1, 5].
  • • Semester Farewell Meetings: Dedicated community celebrations and milestone send-offs held in December 2025 and April 2026 to honor graduating scholars[cite: 1, 5].
  • • IBM-HBCU Quantum Symposium: Cohort delegations engaging with national quantum computing mentors and HBCU peers in Chicago, IL[cite: 4, 8].
